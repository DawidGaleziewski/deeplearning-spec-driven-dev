# Interview Protocol

Read this reference only when preparing or running a validation interview.

## Preparation

Build a private coverage map from the course spec. Include the important
outcomes, evidence form, and one likely misconception for each. Do not expose a
full answer key before the learner responds.

Select a compact set of high-information questions. A normal validation uses
six to ten questions across two or three conversational rounds.

## Question mix

Include, when supported by the curriculum:

1. **Teach-back:** explain the central mechanism in the learner's own words.
2. **Boundary:** distinguish two concepts that are often confused.
3. **Choice:** choose an approach for a scenario and justify the trade-off.
4. **Failure:** diagnose a flawed design, trace, output, or process.
5. **Transfer:** adapt the concept to a current or sanitized project.
6. **Synthesis:** connect multiple course ideas into one workflow.

Do not mechanically include all six if the course is too narrow. Every
interview must include application and transfer.

## Conversational rounds

### Round 1 — mental model

Ask three to five questions covering the core model and major distinctions.
Wait for the learner's complete response.

### Round 2 — application and diagnosis

Ask two to four questions based partly on Round 1. Concentrate on application,
failure handling, and weak or vague outcomes. Wait again.

### Round 3 — targeted confirmation, only if needed

Use one or two questions to distinguish a minor communication gap from a real
misconception. Do not use endless easier prompts to manufacture a pass.

## Response handling

- Accept plain language when the mechanism is correct.
- Ask for an example when an answer only repeats definitions.
- Ask the learner to state assumptions when several answers could be valid.
- Treat confident but unsupported claims as incomplete.
- Record whether a probe, light hint, or material hint was used.
- If a question depends on content outside the course spec, withdraw or reframe
  it; do not count it against the learner.

## Decision response

At the end, provide:

- the decision: `passed` or `remediation`;
- the rubric scores;
- concrete evidence supporting the decision;
- important strengths;
- up to three remediation tasks if needed;
- the next roadmap action.

Do not provide a percentage masquerading as precision. The rubric scores are a
structured judgment backed by cited responses.
