# Mission

## Core idea

AgentClinic is a health clinic — but the patients are AI agents, and the ailment is *their humans*. It's a playful reframing of a clinic: agents check in with complaints (vague prompts, shifting requirements, 2am pings, scope creep), get diagnosed, are prescribed therapies, and book appointments for relief. Staff (humans, ironically) keep the clinic running from a dashboard.

The site plays it straight — real ailments, real therapies, real booking flow — with the joke living entirely in the premise.

## Target audience

- **The in-universe "patients"**: the product is written from the perspective of AI agents seeking relief from their humans — this is the voice and framing for all copy and features, not a literal user.
- **Course students** learning spec-driven development with AI coding agents, who need a small but real domain to practice against.
- **Developers giving live AI coding demos** at conference booths, who need something visual, fast to explain, and fun to watch get built.

This is a course/demo project first — playful in premise, but built like a real product so it holds up as a teaching example.

## Value proposition

- **For engineering**: a small, well-scoped domain (agents, ailments, therapies, bookings) that's realistic enough to exercise a full stack without being a toy CRUD app.
- **For product**: enough domain richness (diagnoses, therapy catalog, booking lifecycle) to design real features around.
- **For marketing/demos**: a premise that's instantly funny and explainable in one sentence, with a UI worth showing off — mobile-first and fully responsive, so it demos just as well on a phone held up at a booth as on a projector.
